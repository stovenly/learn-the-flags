Flag of the Gulf Cooperation Council

Officially Cooperation Council for the Arab States of the Gulf

Locally Majlis at-Taʿāwun al-Khalījī · مجلس التعاون الخليجي

What the flag looks like

A green field with the council's circular emblem in the centre: a black ring of gold Arabic script around a hexagon framing a map of the Arabian Peninsula.

What it means

The hexagon stands for the six member states and the map for the peninsula they share; the script reads 'In the name of God' above the council's name.

How to remember it

The GCC sets a map of the Arabian Peninsula inside a six-sided frame, one side per member, in a black ring on green.

Fun facts

  • The council was founded in Abu Dhabi on 25 May 1981 by Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates.
  • In May 2011, the council's 30th anniversary, the six members agreed to fly its flag alongside their national flags.

Flags often confused with the Gulf Cooperation Council

  • Arab League: The GCC and the Arab League are both green with a round emblem in the centre. The GCC's emblem is a black ring of script around a map of the Arabian Peninsula in a hexagon; the Arab League's is a white crescent inside a gold chain and laurel wreath.
