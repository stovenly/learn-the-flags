Flag of Newfoundland and Labrador

Officially Province of Newfoundland and Labrador

What the flag looks like

A mostly white flag with blue triangles and rectangles at the hoist, and in the fly two red triangles edged in gold with a gold arrow between them.

What it means

The blue section recalls the Union Jack; white stands for snow and ice, blue for the sea, red for human effort and gold for confidence, while the arrow points towards a brighter future.

How to remember it

Newfoundland and Labrador is an angular, mostly white flag: blue triangles at the hoist and a red-and-gold arrow shape in the fly, with no shield or emblem at all.

Fun facts

  • The flag was designed by the Newfoundland painter Christopher Pratt and first flown on 24 June 1980.
  • Newfoundland joined Canada in 1949, the last province to do so.
