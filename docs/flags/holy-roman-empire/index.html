Flag of the Holy Roman Empire

Heiliges Römisches Reich

What the flag looks like

A gold field with a black double-headed eagle, each head surrounded by a halo, with red beaks, tongues and talons.

What it means

Black and gold were the imperial colours, and the double-headed eagle became the emblem of the emperor in the 15th century, while a single-headed eagle stood for the King of the Romans.

How to remember it

The Holy Roman Empire's banner is a black two-headed eagle with haloes on gold; Albania's black two-headed eagle sits on red, and Prussia's single-headed eagle on white.

Fun facts

  • Voltaire quipped that the Holy Roman Empire was "neither holy, nor Roman, nor an empire".
  • The empire was dissolved in 1806, when Francis II laid down the crown after Napoleon's victories; he continued to reign as Emperor of Austria.

Flags often confused with the Holy Roman Empire

  • Albania: The Holy Roman Empire and Albania both show a black double-headed eagle. The Holy Roman Empire's eagle is on gold and has haloes; Albania's is on red.
  • Prussia: The Holy Roman Empire and Prussia both show a black eagle. The Holy Roman Empire's eagle has two haloed heads on gold; Prussia's has one crowned head on white, between thin black stripes at the top and bottom.
