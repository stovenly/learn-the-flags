Flag of England

What the flag looks like

A white field with a red cross reaching to the edges, the Cross of St George.

What it means

The red cross on white is the emblem of St George, England's patron saint, and was in use as an English symbol by the late Middle Ages.

How to remember it

England is the plain red cross on white: add four little crosses and you get Georgia, lay a gold cross over it and you get Guernsey.

Fun facts

  • The red St George's cross forms the upright cross at the centre of the Union Jack.
  • Genoa and Milan also use a red cross on white as their flag.

Flags often confused with England

  • Guernsey: England and Guernsey are both a red St George's cross on white. England's flag is plain; Guernsey lays a smaller gold cross over the red one.
  • Georgia: England and Georgia are both a large red cross on white. England's flag is plain; Georgia adds four small red crosses in the corners.
  • Alderney: England and Alderney both have the red cross of St George on white. England's is plain; Alderney adds a green disc with a gold lion at the centre.
  • Sark: England and Sark both have the red cross of St George on white. England's is plain; Sark adds a red canton with two gold lions.
  • Sardinia: England and Sardinia both have a red cross on white. England's flag is plain; Sardinia adds a black Moor's head in each quarter.
