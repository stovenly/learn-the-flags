Flag of Canada

What the flag looks like

A red vertical band at each end, each a quarter of the width, with a white square in the centre holding a red stylised 11-point maple leaf.

What it means

Red and white were proclaimed Canada's official colours in 1921, and the maple leaf has long been a national emblem.

How to remember it

Canada is red-white-red standing up with one 11-point maple leaf on a wide white square; Peru has no leaf and three equal bands.

Fun facts

  • The flag was first raised on 15 February 1965, now marked as National Flag of Canada Day.
  • The stylised maple leaf has 11 points, although real maple leaves usually have more.
  • Canada has the longest coastline of any country in the world.

Flags often confused with Canada

  • Peru: Canada and Peru both have vertical red-white-red bands. Peru's three bands are equal with no maple leaf; Canada's white centre is twice as wide and holds a red maple leaf.
  • Lebanon: Canada and Lebanon are both red and white with a plant emblem in the centre. Canada's red bands are vertical at the sides with a red maple leaf; Lebanon's are horizontal with a green cedar.
