Flag of Kansas

Officially State of Kansas

What the flag looks like

A dark blue field with the state seal in the centre, a sunflower on a twisted blue and gold bar above it, and KANSAS in gold letters below.

What it means

The seal shows a settler ploughing, a wagon train and bison under a rising sun, with 34 stars because Kansas was the 34th state.

How to remember it

Kansas is the seal-on-blue with a sunflower perched on top and KANSAS in gold underneath.

Fun facts

  • The seal's motto "Ad astra per aspera" means "To the stars through difficulties".
  • The flag was adopted in 1927; the word KANSAS was added in 1961.

Flags often confused with Kansas

  • Nebraska: Kansas and Nebraska are both a state seal on dark blue. Kansas has a sunflower above the seal and KANSAS below; Nebraska's seal is plain gold and silver with no name underneath.
  • Montana: Kansas and Montana both put a seal and the state's name in gold on blue. Kansas has KANSAS below and a sunflower on top; Montana has MONTANA above the seal.
