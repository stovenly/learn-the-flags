Flag of the Cook Islands

Kūki 'Āirani

Territories

What the flag looks like

A dark blue field with the Union Jack in the upper hoist corner and a ring of fifteen white five-pointed stars in the fly.

What it means

The fifteen stars stand for the fifteen islands, and the ring for the unity of their people; the Union Jack recalls the link with the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth.

How to remember it

The Cook Islands set their stars in a circle: fifteen white stars in a ring, one per island, where New Zealand and Australia scatter theirs as the Southern Cross.

Fun facts

  • From 1973 to 1979 the flag was green with a ring of fifteen golden stars.
  • Cook Islanders are New Zealand citizens, and far more of them live in New Zealand than in the islands.

Flags often confused with Cook Islands

New ZealandThe Cook Islands and New Zealand are both dark blue with the Union Jack in the corner. The Cook Islands have fifteen white stars in a ring; New Zealand has four red stars with white edges in the Southern Cross.AustraliaAustralia and the Cook Islands are both dark blue with the Union Jack and white stars. Australia's stars form the Southern Cross with a large star under the Jack; the Cook Islands' fifteen stars form a ring.AnguillaAnguilla and the Cook Islands are both Blue Ensigns with a circle in the fly. Anguilla's circle is three orange dolphins on a white and turquoise shield; the Cook Islands' is a ring of fifteen white stars straight on the blue.
