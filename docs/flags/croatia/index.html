Flag of Croatia

Officially Republic of Croatia

Locally Hrvatska

What the flag looks like

Three equal horizontal stripes of red, white and blue, with the coat of arms in the centre: a red-and-white checkerboard shield topped by a crown of five small historical shields.

What it means

Red, white and blue are pan-Slavic colours; the checkerboard is Croatia's traditional arms, and the five crown shields represent historic regions including Dubrovnik, Dalmatia, Istria and Slavonia.

How to remember it

Red-white-blue with a red-and-white chessboard shield in the middle: Croatia plays chess, while the Netherlands is plain and Serbia runs red-blue-white.

Fun facts

  • The checkerboard shield, called the šahovnica, has been a Croatian symbol since at least the 15th century.
  • The necktie takes its name, 'cravat', from the scarves worn by Croatian soldiers in 17th-century France.

Flags often confused with Croatia

  • Netherlands: Croatia and the Netherlands are both red, white and blue horizontal stripes. Croatia has a red-and-white checkerboard shield in the centre; the Netherlands is plain.
  • Paraguay: Croatia and Paraguay are both red, white and blue with a central emblem. Croatia has a large checkerboard shield spilling over the stripes; Paraguay has a small round seal inside the white band.
  • Serbia: Croatia and Serbia both use red, white and blue with a coat of arms. Croatia's order is red-white-blue with a checkerboard shield; Serbia's is red-blue-white with an eagle shield towards the hoist.
  • Luxembourg: Croatia and Luxembourg are both red, white and blue. Croatia has a large checkerboard shield in the centre; Luxembourg is plain with a lighter blue.
