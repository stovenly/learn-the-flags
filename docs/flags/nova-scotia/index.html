Flag of Nova Scotia

Province of Nova Scotia

Nouvelle-Écosse

What the flag looks like

A blue saltire on a white field, with a gold shield bearing a red rampant lion at the centre.

What it means

The saltire is Scotland's St Andrew's cross with its colours reversed, and the shield bears the royal arms of Scotland, recalling the colony granted by King James VI to Sir William Alexander in 1621.

How to remember it

Nova Scotia means "New Scotland", so it flips Scotland's flag: a blue X on white, with Scotland's red lion on a gold shield where the arms cross.

Fun facts

  • The coat of arms the flag is based on was granted in 1625, making it the oldest provincial coat of arms in Canada.
  • The flag was used from 1858 but was only confirmed in provincial law in 2013.
