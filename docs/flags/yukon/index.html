Flag of Yukon

What the flag looks like

Three vertical panels of green, white and blue, the white center wider, with the territorial coat of arms above a wreath of fireweed in the center.

What it means

Green stands for the forests, white for the snow and blue for the lakes and rivers; fireweed is the territorial flower.

How to remember it

Yukon runs green, white and blue, with a coat of arms topped by a husky-like malamute on the white; the Northwest Territories have blue on both sides.

Fun facts

  • The flag was picked from a territory-wide design competition in 1967 and adopted on 1 March 1968.
  • The crest of the coat of arms on the flag is a malamute dog standing on a mound of snow.

Flags often confused with Yukon

  • Northwest Territories: The Northwest Territories and Yukon both have a wide white center with an emblem between colored side panels. The Northwest Territories have blue on both sides and a plain shield; Yukon has green at the hoist, blue at the fly and a full coat of arms over fireweed.
