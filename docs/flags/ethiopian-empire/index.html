Flag of the Ethiopian Empire

Ityop'iya · ኢትዮጵያ

What the flag looks like

Three equal horizontal stripes of green, yellow and red, with the crowned Lion of Judah in the centre, carrying a staff topped by a cross and a small pennant in the same colours.

What it means

The Lion of Judah stood for the emperors, who claimed descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.

How to remember it

Imperial Ethiopia's green-yellow-red flag has a crowned lion carrying a cross-topped staff; today's Ethiopia swaps the lion for a blue disc and star, and Bolivia has the stripes the other way up.

Fun facts

  • Ghana borrowed Ethiopia's red, gold and green for its flag in 1957, and so many newly independent states followed that they became known as the pan-African colours.
  • The lion flag remains a symbol of the Rastafari movement, which reveres Emperor Haile Selassie.

Flags often confused with the Ethiopian Empire

  • Ethiopia: The Ethiopian Empire and Ethiopia are both green, yellow and red horizontal stripes. The Empire has a crowned lion carrying a staff; Ethiopia today has a blue disc with a yellow star.
  • Bolivia: The Ethiopian Empire and Bolivia both have horizontal stripes of red, yellow and green. Bolivia has red on top; the Ethiopian Empire has green on top and a crowned lion in the centre.
