Flag of Brittany

Breizh

What the flag looks like

Nine horizontal stripes, five black alternating with four white, and a white canton scattered with black ermine spots.

What it means

The five black stripes stand for the traditionally Gallo-speaking dioceses of Upper Brittany and the four white ones for the Breton-speaking dioceses of Lower Brittany; the ermine comes from the arms of the Duchy of Brittany.

How to remember it

Brittany is Uruguay's nine-stripe layout in black and white, with ermine tails in the corner instead of a sun.

Fun facts

  • The Gwenn-ha-du, Breton for "white and black", was designed by Morvan Marchal in 1923; it has no official status but is flown widely, including on many town halls.
  • It is also flown in Loire-Atlantique, around Nantes, which belonged to historic Brittany but lies outside today's region.

Flags often confused with Brittany

  • Uruguay: Brittany and Uruguay both have nine horizontal stripes and a white canton. Uruguay's stripes are white and blue with a gold sun; Brittany's are black and white with black ermine spots.
  • United States: Brittany and the United States both have horizontal stripes with a decorated canton. The US flag is red, white and blue with 13 stripes and stars; Brittany is black and white with nine stripes and ermine spots.
