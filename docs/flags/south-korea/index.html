Flag of South Korea

Officially Republic of Korea

Locally Hanguk · 한국

What the flag looks like

A white field with a red-and-blue taegeuk (yin-yang) circle in the centre, red on top, and a black trigram of three broken or unbroken bars near each corner.

What it means

White stands for peace and purity, the taegeuk for the balance of opposing forces, and the four trigrams for heaven, earth, water and fire.

How to remember it

South Korea is a white flag with a red-and-blue yin-yang circle and four black bar patterns in the corners; North Korea has stripes and a red star.

Fun facts

  • The flag is called the Taegukgi, and its trigrams come from the ancient Chinese divination text the I Ching.
  • A version of the flag was first used in 1883 by the Joseon dynasty.
