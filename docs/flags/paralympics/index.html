Flag of the Paralympics

Officially Paralympic flag

What the flag looks like

A white field with three crescent-shaped swooshes, red, blue and green, curving around a central point.

What it means

The three agitos (Latin for 'I move') stand for movement and the spirit of Paralympic athletes; red, blue and green are the colours most widely found in national flags.

How to remember it

The Paralympic flag has three swooshes, red, blue and green, swirling round an empty centre; the Olympic flag has five interlocking rings.

Fun facts

  • The first Paralympic logo, made for the 1988 Seoul Games, used five Korean-style teardrop shapes arranged like the Olympic rings.
  • The three-agitos design was introduced in 2004 and refreshed in 2019 with brighter colours matching the Olympic rings.

Flags often confused with the Paralympics

  • Olympics: The Olympic and Paralympic flags are both white with coloured symbols in the centre. The Olympics have five interlocking rings; the Paralympics have three swooshes in red, blue and green.
