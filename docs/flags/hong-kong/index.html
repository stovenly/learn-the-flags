Flag of Hong Kong

Officially Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China

Locally Hēung Góng · 香港

What the flag looks like

A red field with a stylised white five-petalled bauhinia flower in the centre, each petal carrying a small red five-pointed star.

What it means

Red is the colour of the Chinese national flag and the white bauhinia stands for Hong Kong, so the two colours together express "one country, two systems"; the stars echo those on China's flag.

How to remember it

Hong Kong is a white bauhinia flower on red, each of its five petals holding a red star; Macau swaps it for a white lotus on green.

Fun facts

  • The flag was first raised officially at midnight on 1 July 1997, at the ceremony handing Hong Kong from the United Kingdom to China.
  • The flower, Bauhinia × blakeana, is a sterile hybrid first found in Hong Kong and can only be grown from cuttings or grafts.

Flags often confused with Hong Kong

  • Macau: Hong Kong and Macau both have a white flower in the centre of a single-coloured field. Hong Kong has a five-petalled bauhinia on red; Macau has a lotus on green, under five yellow stars.
  • China: China and Hong Kong both have a red field with five-pointed stars. China has yellow stars in the upper hoist corner; Hong Kong has a white bauhinia flower in the centre with small red stars on its petals.
