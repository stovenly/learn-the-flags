Flag of Liberia

Republic of Liberia

What the flag looks like

Eleven horizontal stripes alternating red and white, with a blue square canton in the upper hoist bearing a single white five-pointed star.

What it means

The eleven stripes stand for the signatories of Liberia's Declaration of Independence; the lone star represents Liberia as Africa's first independent republic.

How to remember it

Liberia is the 'Lone Star' flag: US-style red and white stripes but only eleven of them, and a single white star in the blue square.

Fun facts

  • Liberia was founded by the American Colonization Society as a home for freed African Americans, and its capital Monrovia is named after US President James Monroe.
  • Liberia runs one of the world's largest ship registries, so many ocean-going ships fly its flag.

Flags often confused with Liberia

  • United States: Liberia and the United States both have red and white stripes with a blue canton. Liberia's canton holds just one large white star; the US canton holds 50 small stars.
  • Malaysia: Liberia and Malaysia both have red and white stripes with a blue canton. Liberia has a single white star; Malaysia has a yellow crescent and a yellow 14-pointed star.
  • Togo: Liberia and Togo both have horizontal stripes and a square canton with one white star. Liberia is red and white with a blue canton; Togo is green and yellow with a red canton.
  • Chile: Chile and Liberia both have one white star in a blue square in the corner. Chile has just two bands, white over red; Liberia has eleven red and white stripes.
