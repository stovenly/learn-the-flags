Flag of Corsica

Corse

Collectivity of Corsica · Territories

What the flag looks like

A white field with a black Moor's head in profile, facing the hoist, wearing a white headband tied at the back of the head.

What it means

The Moor's head is an old Corsican emblem; Pasquale Paoli is said to have raised the band from the Moor's eyes to his forehead as a sign of the island's liberation.

How to remember it

Corsica is a single Moor's head alone on white; Sardinia shows four of them, split by a red cross.

Fun facts

  • Pasquale Paoli made it the flag of the independent Corsican Republic in 1755; it fell out of use after France took the island and was re-adopted as the regional flag in 1980.
  • Napoleon Bonaparte was born in Ajaccio in 1769, the year French troops completed their conquest of the island.

Flags often confused with Corsica

  • Sardinia: Corsica and Sardinia both show a black Moor's head with a white headband on white. Corsica has a single large head and no cross; Sardinia has four smaller heads separated by a red cross.
